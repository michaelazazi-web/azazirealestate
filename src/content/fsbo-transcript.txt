If you've decided to sell on your own, you probably had a good reason. Maybe you want to save the commission. Maybe you've bought and sold homes before and feel confident doing it again. Or maybe you just didn't want to deal with pushy agents chasing a listing. Honestly, I respect that.

But most owners who sell themselves run into a few unexpected challenges.

The first is showings. Suddenly your phone is ringing with buyers, agents, and people who are just curious about the house.

Then there's pricing. Setting the right price isn't just about what similar homes sold for. It's about how buyers see your home compared to their other options right now.

And then comes negotiation. The buyer usually has an experienced agent on their side whose job is to get the best possible deal for their client. That's the part most homeowners don't see coming.

Here's something most agents won't say. Not because they're bad people, but because a lot of agents approach For Sale By Owner sellers the wrong way. They assume you're doing something wrong, or they immediately push you to list.

The reality is, many homeowners are very capable of selling their own home. The real challenge isn't the ability to sell. It's something called market positioning.

A home doesn't sell simply because it's listed. It sells because it becomes the most attractive option for buyers in that price range. That's why two very similar homes can have completely different outcomes. One becomes the obvious choice. The other becomes just another option.

Most owner-sold homes follow the same path. The home goes online. Showings start. Buyers compare it to everything else they're seeing. And eventually an offer comes in, often below asking. Not because the home isn't good, but because buyers are comparing it to everything else available.

When a home is positioned strategically, the process looks very different. Here's how I approach it.

Step one: reaching the buyers who are actually out there. Most buyers today shop with an agent, and many only see homes that appear in the MLS and the major sites it feeds. A yard sign and a single online listing reach only a slice of them. The fewer buyers who see your home, the less leverage you have.

Step two: positioning against today's competition. Sold homes tell you what happened months ago. What matters is what buyers in your price range are choosing between right now, and what they're willing to pay for. That's what tells us how to price and present your home so it stands out instead of blending in.

Step three: screening buyers before they walk in. Not every caller is a qualified buyer. Confirming financing and seriousness up front protects your time, your privacy, and your safety, and keeps you from tying up your home with a buyer who can't close.

Step four: creating buyer competition. When buyers feel like they're the only one interested, they negotiate. When they feel other buyers may want the home too, they act faster and stronger.

Step five: protecting the deal all the way to closing. Getting an offer is only halfway. Inspections, repair requests, and the appraisal are where many owner sales lose money or fall apart. Knowing how to handle each one is what protects the price you agreed to.
